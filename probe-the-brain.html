Key idea — Motor area of the cerebrum

The motor area of the cerebrum (motor cortex) initiates voluntary body movements. Body parts that need fine, precise control — such as the fingers, lips and tongue — are allocated a much larger area in the motor cortex because they need a higher density of motor neurones for delicate control.

Blue area = Sensory map (feels touch) | Red area = Motor map (controls movement).

Left Brain → Right Body

Nerve signals cross over inside the central nervous system. The left motor area controls muscles on the right side of the body.

Right Brain → Left Body

Likewise, the right motor area controls muscles on the left side of the body. This opposite-side rule is called cross-control (or contralateral control).

1 · Probe the motor area

Move the probe tip over a red motor-area label — movement keeps going while you stay there. Move away to stop. You can also use the quick buttons under the diagrams.

2 · Body response

Watch which side of the body moves. It should be the opposite side to the motor area under the probe tip.

What is happening in this simulation?

Move the probe tip over a motor-area label to begin…

How to explore: Slide the probe tip across the red motor area. Each label stimulates the matching body part on the opposite side of the body (cross-control). While the tip stays on a label the movement continues; move off and it stops.
Note on diagram representation: Some regions (for example, the fingers) are drawn larger to suggest that fine, precise control needs more motor cortex. However, the box sizes in this simulation are only a simplified guide and may not match the true proportions of the motor cortex in real biology.
