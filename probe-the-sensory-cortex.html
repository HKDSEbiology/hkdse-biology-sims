Key idea — Sensory area of the cerebrum

The sensory area of the cerebrum (sensory cortex) receives nerve impulses from receptors in different parts of the body and produces sensations (for example, pain, touch or temperature). Body parts with fine sensitivity (such as the fingers, lips and tongue) have a larger representation in the sensory cortex.

Blue band = Sensory cortex (receives sensations) | Red band = Motor cortex (controls movement).

Right Body → Left Brain

Sensory pathways also cross over. A stimulus on the right side of the body is mainly processed by the left sensory area of the cerebrum.

Left Body → Right Brain

Likewise, a stimulus on the left side of the body is mainly processed by the right sensory area. This opposite-side rule is called cross-control (or contralateral control).

What is happening in this simulation?

Move the mosquito tip over a body region to begin…

How to explore: Slide the mosquito tip across the body map. Each body region sends a signal to the sensory cortex on the opposite side of the cerebrum. Move off a target region and the pain sensation stops.
Note on diagram representation: Some regions (for example, the fingers) are drawn larger to suggest higher sensitivity. The box sizes in this simulation are only a simplified guide and may not match the true proportions of the sensory cortex in real biology.
